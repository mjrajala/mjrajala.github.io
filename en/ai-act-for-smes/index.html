AI Act

The AI Act does not ban AI. It forces companies to manage it.

For an SME, the most important task is not memorizing the whole regulation. It is knowing where AI is used, for what purpose, with which data, and under whose responsibility.

Checked on 25 May 2026 against the latest information from the European Commission, the AI Act Service Desk, and the Council of the EU.

Timeline

  • 1 Aug 2024: the AI Act entered into force.
  • 2 Feb 2025: prohibited practices and AI literacy obligations started to apply.
  • 2 Aug 2025: obligations for GPAI model providers and the governance structure started to apply.
  • 2 Aug 2026: most other rules start to apply, including general transparency obligations and market surveillance.
  • 2 Dec 2027: under the political agreement, rules for stand-alone high-risk AI systems start to apply.
  • 2 Aug 2028: under the political agreement, rules for high-risk AI systems embedded in products start to apply.

Management minimum checklist

  • List the AI tools and agents in use.
  • Record purpose, users, data, and decision authority.
  • Identify whether use relates to HR, biometrics, critical infrastructure, education, or other higher-risk areas.
  • Define where a human reviews the output.
  • Store instructions, logs, and changes for later audit.
