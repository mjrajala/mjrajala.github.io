What a local model does

Fit is assessed against your tasks and hardware.

Local models come in many sizes. A suitable one handles a bounded use case well, for example questions and summaries about material you don’t want to send to an outside service. What works locally for you, and what is better done another way, becomes clear once we go through the tasks and the hardware you have. Cloud models such as Claude or ChatGPT do not run locally; they are connected over their APIs.

When the processing is configured to stay fully local, the material stays on your devices. If cloud integrations are included, selected data is transferred out through them. Those integrations are always agreed separately, and we document what moves. Data protection doesn’t end there: it also depends on how your machines, access rights and backups are handled. We go through those as part of the deployment. Regulated and classified environments are always assessed separately against the requirements that apply to them.

When data must stay inside your organisation

  • HR: internal questions and summaries from salary and personnel records
  • Finance: working through unpublished forecasts, cash flow and M&A calculations
  • Legal: searching and summarising confidential contracts and case material
  • Healthcare: patient record summaries for a professional to review
  • R&D: searching your own technical documents and trade secrets
  • Defence: handling military operational or technical documents in an isolated, approved environment

Decide before installing

  1. Which work the model is used for first.
  2. Which documents the model may use.
  3. Whether a suitable machine exists or needs buying.
  4. Who maintains and updates the model.
