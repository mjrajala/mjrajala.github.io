WritingAI agents19 Aug 2026

Models keep getting better. Their writing is getting easier to spot.

Models write more fluently than they did a year ago. Their default voice is also easier to recognise. That is a bad mix if the agent is talking to a customer.

Ask a model for copy and it often sounds finished at once. Too finished. The sentences are even, the rhythm is predictable and every paragraph tries to sound important.

Once you see the style, you keep seeing it. Too many em dashes. Perfectly tidy lists of three. Dramatic sentence fragments. Metaphors doing overtime to explain something simple.

The text can still be factually fine. It just sounds like a machine wrote it. When a reader starts noticing the pattern, the conversation loses its grip.

Claude-speak is not just a taste issue

Mason Hall wrote this week that he is tired of so-called Claude-speak. Nous Research answered with a practical suggestion: try the Hermes Humanizer skill.

A short exchange, but it hits the right point. The problem is not that the model cannot write. The problem is that it writes in the same way every time.

That is why a stronger model does not automatically fix voice. Voice is an editorial decision: what to leave out, when to say the thing directly, and whose mouth the text should come from.

Intelligence is only part of the product

An agent is not valuable because it produces more text. It is valuable when the text sounds like the person or company it is speaking for.

The model's default style is even, polite and a little too ready. A company's voice is usually less tidy. It has familiar words, short sentences, and sometimes a bluntness that would never survive a generic prompt.

You do not get that by asking it to "write more human". That usually swaps one mannerism for another. Warm metaphors arrive, the transitions get softer, and the new pattern is just as easy to spot.

The customer notices the pattern first

This matters most at the customer edge. A reply can be completely correct and still feel like a mass message. The first one may pass. By the third, the reader is already waiting for the next tidy list.

Trust does not fail because of a wrong fact. It thins out because the message does not sound like anyone.

The same thing happens on a company's own social channels. If every post sounds like the same model, people do not remember you. They remember the style.

A better first test: do not ask it to "write a post". Give it three of your own texts, a list of words and habits to avoid, and an instruction to edit the draft before anyone else sees it.

Give the agent a voice, not just a task

This is briefing, not magic. A few real samples beat a long personality description.

Give the agent actual examples of how you write. An email, a customer reply or a published post tells it more than "be relaxed but expert".

Name the words and habits it should avoid. If you hate em dashes, "unlock", "a new era" and three-point sermons, put them on a blocklist.

Ask it to edit the first draft before publishing. The first version is usually where the model's default voice is loudest.

And check that it does not invent opinions, experiences or unearned certainty. The most revealing AI sentence is not always the style. It is the line where the model sounds like a seasoned person without having earned that voice.

The next skill is leaving things out

We spent years teaching AI to produce more text. Next it has to learn what is worth cutting.

A good agent does not fill the page. It says the thing, cuts the extra and sounds the same tomorrow as it does today.

At that point the model is only the engine. The voice is the product.
