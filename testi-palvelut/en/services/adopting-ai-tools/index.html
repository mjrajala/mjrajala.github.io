Services / Adopting AI tools

Off-the-shelf AI tools, adopted properly.

ChatGPT, Claude and Grok already exist, and they get you surprisingly far. The hard part isn’t picking one. It is getting everyone to use it the same way, and getting it connected to your own information. That is where we help.

What we do

  • Chosen for the work

    We don’t start from the tool, we start from the job. Once it is clear what the AI is for, there are usually two or three options, not twenty.

    • A review of what the work is and how many people need it
    • The options, and what each one asks of you
    • The plans, and what is genuinely included in each

    • Which interfaces the tool has, and which it lacks
    • Scoped access to your documents and systems
    • What data goes out to the tool and what does not

The most common misunderstanding

A browser subscription is not the same thing as automation.

Your people having a ChatGPT or Claude subscription in the browser does not mean you can build automation on that subscription. A chat interface and a programmatic interface are different things: often a different product, different pricing and different terms of use.

In some tools the interface exists and is documented. In some it belongs only to a business-tier plan. In some there is none at all, and then automation has to be built another way. This is cheap to establish up front and expensive to discover afterwards.

Availability also varies by region and by plan, and it changes from month to month. So we check the current position at the start of a project instead of trusting what was true last spring.

What we don’t promise

  • We don’t promise that a particular tool or feature is available to you until it has been checked for your region and your plan.
  • We don’t rank the tools by performance numbers. Those assessments go stale faster than they can be written down.
  • We don’t publish a price. You pay the licences directly to the vendor, and our own work is priced from the scope.

What we check about plans

  • Whether a programmatic interface is included or is a separate product
  • What the terms of use say about your company’s data
  • Whether the feature is available in your region
  • Who administers accounts, and what happens when someone leaves
  • What changing plan later would involve

Decide before adoption

  1. Which work the tool is used for first.
  2. Who gets accounts, and on which plan.
  3. What material may be pasted into the tool and what may not.
  4. Who owns the guidance and keeps it up to date.

The tools customers usually ask about

These are general-purpose chat tools that we use ourselves and get asked about. We won’t tell you here which one is better, because the answer depends on the work and goes stale. We’ll tell you during scoping, once we know what the tool is for.

Claude

Grok

Dots
