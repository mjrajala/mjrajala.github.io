AI agents and AI services for business.

There are two ways to get value out of AI. You adopt the tools that already exist and get them working inside your systems and your way of doing things. Or we build you an agent, an assistant or software of your own, when an off-the-shelf tool isn’t enough. We do both, and we start with whichever comes out cheaper for you.

Two routes

An existing tool, or one of your own?

Usually it pays to start with what exists. ChatGPT, Claude and Grok get you surprisingly far, and adopting them doesn’t require a development project. Something of your own pays off when the requirements can’t be met with the tools available.

Plenty of off-the-shelf tools can work with your own data and run tasks with nobody sitting at the machine. Whether yours can depends on the product, the plan and the interfaces, so that gets checked before anything is built from scratch.

You rarely need only one. The common order is that the off-the-shelf tools go in first, and once recurring work shows up inside them, that work gets built into an agent.

Adopt the existing tools

We help you choose the tool, get onto the right plan, write guidance that fits your company, and connect the tool to your systems as far as that is actually possible.

Usually a lighter start than building your own. The timeline depends on which accounts, access rights and connections are needed.

An agent or assistant of your own

We build an agent that does your work inside your systems, with agreed access rights and agreed approval points.

Worth it when an off-the-shelf tool can’t give you what you need: tighter scoping, stricter access rights, or a deeper connection to your systems.

The differences, briefly

An assistant answers. An agent acts. An integration opens the door.

These three get mixed up, and the confusion shows at the start of a project: someone buys an assistant when what they needed was an agent. The difference is easiest to see in what you are left holding and who presses the button.

A private AI server is not an alternative to these. It is a decision about where the model runs, and it can sit underneath any of them.

Knowledge assistant

You get: an answer and its source.

Triggered by: a person asking.

Changes: nothing. Read-only.

Fits when: the information exists but finding it takes too long.

AI agent

You get: finished work and a log of what was done.

Triggered by: a task, a schedule or an incoming message.

Changes: yes, within agreed limits and after approval.

Fits when: the work needs doing, not explaining.

AI integration

You get: a working connection to a system.

Triggered by: whichever agent, assistant or tool needs the data.

Changes: only the fields that were opened on purpose.

Fits when: the data is in a system the AI cannot reach yet.

How a delivery runs

  1. ScopingWe go through which work is involved, where the data comes from and who owns the result. After that we know whether an off-the-shelf tool is enough or an agent of your own is needed.
  2. Build or set-upWe build or deploy a bounded version and test it with your own data. Approval points and access rights are agreed before production.
  3. Go-live and supportTraining for the users, logging and monitoring switched on, and a written agreement on who updates what and what support covers.

The price depends on the scope, so it is agreed case by case. We don’t publish an up-front figure here, because it would be a guess.
