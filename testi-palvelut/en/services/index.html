The differences, briefly

The first three get mixed up with each other.

They solve different problems, and the confusion shows up right at the start of a project: someone buys an assistant when what they needed was automation, or the other way round. The differences are easiest to see in what each one produces and who makes the decision.

The fourth, a private AI server, is not an alternative to the other three. It is a decision about where the model runs, and it can sit underneath any of them.

Knowledge assistant

Produces: an answer and its source.

Triggered by: a person asking.

Writes: nothing. Read-only.

Fits when: the information exists but finding it takes too long.

Workflow automation

Produces: a finished result and a log.

Triggered by: a schedule, an incoming message or a button.

Writes: yes, within agreed limits and after approval.

Fits when: the same work repeats and the result can be checked.

AI integrations

Produces: a working connection to a system.

Triggered by: whichever assistant, automation or app needs the data.

Writes: only to the fields that were opened on purpose.

Fits when: the data is in a system the AI cannot reach yet.

How a delivery runs

  1. ScopingWe go through which work is involved, where the data comes from and who owns the result. After that we know which service fits and what it needs from you.
  2. BuildWe build a bounded version and test it with your own data. Review points and limits are agreed before anything reaches production.
  3. Go-live and upkeepTraining for the users, logging and monitoring switched on, and an agreement on who updates what and how far upkeep goes.

The price depends on the scope, so it is agreed case by case. We don’t publish an up-front figure here, because it would be a guess.
