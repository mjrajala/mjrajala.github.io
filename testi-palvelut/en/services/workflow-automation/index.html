Services / Workflow automation

Workflow automation: one recurring job at a time.

Automation pays off when the same work is done every week in the same way and the result can be checked. We build one workflow at a time and leave the human at the point where a decision actually has to be made.

Tell us about the workflowAll services

What we do

  • The trigger and the limits

    A workflow needs a clear start and clear limits. Both are written down before anything runs in production.

    • Trigger: a schedule, an incoming message, a new file or a button
    • Which data the workflow may read, and from where
    • What it may not do without approval
    • Where it stops when data is missing or doesn’t match

  • The human review point

    Good automation doesn’t remove the person; it moves them to the right place. Routine passes through, exceptions land on a desk.

    • Approval before an outgoing message or an irreversible entry
    • Exceptions routed to a person, routine handled automatically
    • A rejected draft comes back for correction instead of vanishing
    • Agreed in advance: who approves, and who covers for them
    Guide: AI automation for SMEs

  • Monitoring and upkeep

    An automation nobody watches stops working without anyone noticing. That is why logging and alerts are part of the delivery.

    • A log of what was done, on what data and who approved it
    • An alert when the workflow stalls or gets stuck
    • Throughput and errors followed in an agreed way
    • Changes are versioned, so you can go back

How we deliver

One bounded workflow, all the way to production.

We pick one workflow, agree its tools, limits and review points, and test the implementation with your own data before expanding. That way the automation doesn’t stay a demo.

Expanding is the next decision, not the same project. After the first workflow you know what automation actually demands in your organisation, and you can decide about the rest on real information.

  1. DataDocuments, spreadsheets, systems
  2. AgentFinds, combines and drafts
  3. HumanReviews exceptions and approves
  4. DoneResult and a log of what was done

When automation isn’t worth it

  • The work happens a few times a year. Doing it by hand is cheaper.
  • The work is different every time and cannot be described as an instruction even for a person.
  • The process is broken. Automation also speeds up the wrong outcome.
  • The source data is so unreliable that every case has to be checked anyway.

Examples of good first targets

  • Compiling a weekly report from several sources into the same template
  • Pre-processing and classifying requests for quotation as they arrive
  • A first pass over purchase invoices, with exceptions listed
  • Recurring compliance documentation, where the content changes but the structure doesn’t
  • Background work for customer service: a draft reply and the facts it needs, ready

Decide before building

  1. Which workflow is automated first.
  2. How often it repeats and who does it today.
  3. Where human approval is needed.
  4. How the benefit is measured: time, quality, throughput or reduced risk.
